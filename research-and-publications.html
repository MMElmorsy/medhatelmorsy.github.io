Research & Publications

Experiments that generate validation-grade data, and computational methods that use it — across reinforced concrete, unreinforced masonry, and affordable construction.

Research themes

Benchmark experiments for validating seismic models

Nonlinear models of buildings are calibrated on isolated component tests, then trusted to predict the response of complete systems. Very little experimental data exists at that system scale, and almost none of it is repeated often enough to say anything statistical. I address this by combining geotechnical centrifuge shake table testing with 3D printing, which makes it feasible to build and test many nominally identical small-scale structures. The resulting open datasets support probabilistic validation rather than single-specimen comparison, and were used as the basis of a blind prediction contest run with the Pacific Earthquake Engineering Research Center.

Related work: [J1], blind prediction contest, [C1], [C5]

Additive manufacturing for small-scale reinforced concrete

Reduced-scale models of reinforced concrete have long been limited by the reinforcement: at centrifuge scale, conventional cages cannot be built with realistic geometry or bond. I developed and characterized additively manufactured steel reinforcement together with matched micro-concrete, establishing tensile and bond behavior at the material level and then verifying the approach on columns and beam-column joints under cyclic loading. The method turns small-scale RC testing into a repeatable production process.

Related work: [J4], [J5], [J6], [C2]

Seismic topology optimization and robotic prototyping

Current work at MIT, supported by an SNSF Postdoc.Mobility fellowship. Topology optimization for concrete structures has to respect that the material behaves very differently in tension and compression, and that earthquake demands are dynamic and nonlinear. I am developing a bimodular optimization formulation under seismic loading, together with approximations that keep the nonlinear transient problem tractable inside an optimization loop, and prototyping the resulting layouts physically using additive manufacturing and robotic fabrication.

Funded by the Swiss National Science Foundation · Host: MIT

Affordable earthquake-resistant construction

Most seismic risk sits in buildings that will never be engineered. I work on structural systems that stay within the means of low-income regions: corbel dwellings assembled from interlocking compressed earth blocks, characterized in shear and tested on a centrifuge shake table, and low-cost isolation combining rolling-ball bearings with sand-sandwich layers. The aim is to design the isolation system and the superstructure together rather than adapting expensive technology downward.

Related work: [J2], rolling isolation studies

Modeling uncertainty and its propagation

Beam-column joints are among the least constrained parts of an RC building model, and the choices made there propagate all the way to collapse assessment. I built probabilistic joint models from large experimental databases and quantified how joint modeling uncertainty affects the seismic response of low-rise RC frames, using record-wise Latin hypercube sampling to separate modeling uncertainty from record-to-record variability.

Related work: [J3], [J8], [J9], [J10], [J11]
